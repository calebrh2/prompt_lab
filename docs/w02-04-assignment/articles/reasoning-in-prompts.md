Reasoning in the Prompt
Why this matters on an engagement
Someone will ask to see why the system routed a case the way it did, and the tempting answer is to show them the model's reasoning. It reads well, it sounds like an explanation, and it is the single most dangerous artifact you can put in front of a compliance function. Text a model generates about its own decision is not a record of how the decision was made. If it goes into a case note, you have created an audit trail that describes something that may not have happened, and you will not be able to tell which entries are which.

The nearer problem is cost. Asking a model to reason before answering multiplies the output tokens, and output is the expensive side of the bill. On a task that genuinely needs it, that is money well spent. On field extraction, it is money spent to make the answer slightly worse and considerably slower. Knowing which task you are on is worth more than knowing the technique.

Core concepts
Chain-of-thought asks for intermediate steps, and the steps condition the answer. Nothing changes inside the model. What changes is that the final answer is generated after a sequence of intermediate tokens and attends to them, so a problem that would otherwise have to be resolved in a single step gets resolved across several. That is the whole mechanism, and it explains both where it helps and where it does not. It helps where an answer depends on combining several facts that have to be brought together first. It does nothing where the answer is already present in the input and only needs to be located.

It is paid for in output tokens and in latency. Reasoning is generated text, billed at the output rate, and generation time scales with how many tokens are produced. Day 1's arithmetic applies without modification: a task where reasoning triples the output has roughly tripled the expensive half of its cost, on every call, including the cases that did not need it. This makes reasoning a per-task purchase with a measurable price rather than a setting you turn on because it sounds thorough.

The tasks that pay are the ones with a genuine decision in them. Reconciling a customer narrative against merchant metadata that partly contradicts it. Choosing between two adjacent categories where the distinction turns on several signals. Deciding whether a discrepancy is a conflict worth escalating or a wording difference. The tasks that do not pay are extraction, where the value is in the document and the schema already says what shape it takes, and classification where a single obvious signal decides it. On extraction, reasoning often makes things worse, because it invites the model to elaborate on what a document probably means rather than report what it says. Note one case that looks like a reasoning task and is not: anything that comes down to arithmetic or date computation should be done in code, which is Day 5's subject.

Extended thinking is a provider feature and it is not portable. Several current models expose a mode where the model produces reasoning before its response, managed by the provider rather than by your prompt. The tokens are billed, the latency is real, and what you get back varies: some providers return the trace, some return a summary of it, some return nothing beyond the answer. Availability differs by model and version, so check it for the versions you pinned rather than assuming. Because your harness calls two providers behind one adapter, any design that depends on receiving a reasoning trace is a design that works on one provider and quietly degrades on the other.

If you want reasoning, give it a place in the structure. Reasoning that has nowhere to go ends up inside a value field, and a routing enum contaminated with a paragraph of justification is not a routing enum. Add a dedicated field to the schema, keep it separate from every field a downstream system reads, and decide deliberately whether it is persisted. Persisting it costs storage and creates a record someone will eventually read as though it were authoritative, which brings us to the concept that matters most today.

Reasoning text is not evidence, and it must never be recorded as though it were. The text a model produces about its decision is generated by the same process that produced the decision, and it is not a log of that process. It can be fluent, plausible, internally consistent, and unrelated to what actually drove the output. Research on this is unambiguous: models produce justifications that omit the factors genuinely influencing them. The operational rule follows directly. Evidence is a citation into a source document, which a human can open and check. Reasoning is a token sequence. The first belongs in a case note, in a review packet, and in front of an auditor. The second belongs in a debugging artifact and nowhere else. When you are asked to explain a routing decision, the answer is the cited facts the routing was based on, not the model's account of itself.

Self-consistency buys reliability with multiplied cost, and its disagreement is useful. Sample the same prompt several times at a nonzero temperature and take the majority answer. It improves accuracy on genuinely hard decisions and it costs a full call per sample, so it is reserved for consequential decisions rather than applied by default. The more interesting output is the disagreement itself. Where five samples split three to two, you have a case the model finds genuinely ambiguous, and that is exactly the population you want escalating to a human rather than being resolved by a majority vote. Treating disagreement as a routing signal is usually worth more than treating the majority as an answer.

Worked example
A dispute case where the customer narrative and the merchant metadata do not agree. The customer says they never authorized the charge. The merchant record shows a prior relationship and a delivery confirmation. The two candidate queues are fraud_report and card_dispute, and they lead to different teams and different evidence requirements.

Direct answer, no reasoning requested:

{ "queue": "fraud_report", "requires_human_review": false }

Reasoning requested, with a dedicated field in the schema:

{
  "analysis": "The customer states the charge was unauthorized. The merchant
    record shows two prior completed orders from the same account and a
    delivery confirmation to the address on file. Prior relationship and
    delivery both weigh against an unauthorized transaction and toward a
    dispute about goods or services. The two accounts cannot both be complete.",
  "queue": "card_dispute",
  "requires_human_review": true,
  "review_reason": "Customer account and merchant record conflict on whether a
    prior relationship exists."
}

Across the twelve triage cases, requesting reasoning changed the outcome on three of them, all three of which were cases with conflicting signals. On the nine straightforward cases it changed nothing and added output tokens to every one.

Now the part worth sitting with. The analysis field above is a good piece of writing and it is not evidence. It asserts that the merchant record shows two prior orders. Whether that is true is checkable only against the merchant record, and if the model had that detail wrong, the paragraph would read exactly the same. The review_reason field is different in kind, because it names a conflict a human can go and verify in two named sources. That is the distinction to hold: review_reason can go in a case note, analysis cannot.

The majority is card_dispute, and the majority is not the useful output. Three to two is the system telling you this case does not have a clean answer, on a decision where routing it wrongly sends a possible fraud victim into a goods-and-services process. The right response is to escalate it, which costs one analyst a few minutes, rather than to accept a vote, which costs five calls and produces a confident answer to a question the model could not settle.

Failure modes
Reasoning bought where it was not needed. Applied uniformly, chain-of-thought adds output tokens and latency to every call while changing the answer on a minority of them. On extraction it can actively reduce accuracy by encouraging the model to interpret rather than report. Measure the change on your own cases before adopting it, and adopt it per task.

Reasoning leaking into the value. With no dedicated field, justification arrives inside the field a downstream system reads, and the enum comparison fails or the extracted value carries a sentence of commentary. Give reasoning somewhere to go, or do not ask for it.

Reasoning recorded as evidence. A model's account of its decision written into a case note, a review packet, or a client-facing explanation creates an audit trail that reads as authoritative and describes something that may never have occurred. Evidence is a citation a human can open. Anything else is commentary, and in a regulated setting the difference is not cosmetic.

Self-consistency at temperature zero. Five samples of a near-deterministic configuration produce five near-identical answers, five times the cost, and no additional information. If you are sampling for agreement, you have to be sampling from a distribution wide enough for disagreement to be meaningful, and that decision has to be made deliberately rather than inherited from an extraction prompt.

Assuming the reasoning mode is portable. A design that depends on receiving a reasoning trace works against one provider and silently returns less against another, and because the answer still arrives the degradation looks like a quality difference rather than a missing feature. Anything provider-specific about reasoning belongs behind the adapter boundary, and anything your comparison depends on has to be available from both.

Checklist
[ ] Reasoning is requested per task, on evidence that it changed outcomes on that task's cases.

[ ] The cost of requesting reasoning is measured in output tokens per case, not assumed to be small.

[ ] Reasoning has a dedicated field, separate from every field a downstream system reads.

[ ] A deliberate decision has been made about whether the reasoning field is persisted.

[ ] No reasoning text appears in a case note, a review packet, or any client-facing explanation.

[ ] Anything presented as a justification cites a source a human can open and check.

[ ] Self-consistency, where used, runs at a temperature wide enough for disagreement to be meaningful.

[ ] Disagreement across samples routes the case to a human rather than being resolved by majority.

[ ] No design depends on a reasoning trace that only one of the two providers returns.
